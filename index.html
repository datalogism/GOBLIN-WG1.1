Why this survey

We don't have a shared map of this space

Every knowledge graph and ontology is built through a complex process (design, documentation, generation, transformation, validation, evaluation, migration, reuse) drawing on the whole Semantic Web stack: RML, OWL, SPARQL and, more recently, SHACL. Many methodologies, quality metrics and tools have been proposed along the way.

Yet we know our own tools, and those of our closest collaborators, but nobody has a comparative view of the field, least of all of where human expertise actually does the work versus where it is assumed, documented, or lost.

So we keep re-solving the same problems, and we cannot yet see where the research frontier is, or where agentic and automated approaches should be pointed.

Nobody has a comparative view of…

  • Methodologies: which cover which parts of the lifecycle, where they overlap or leave gaps.
  • Reuse: how shapes, ODPs, modules, ontologies and thesauri are reused, adapted and versioned.
  • Documentation: how design documentation survives (or not) creation, update and migration.
  • Validation: whether reports and logs are connected back to design decisions.
  • Quality: which metrics are used in practice, versus only proposed.
  • Tools: what supports each task, and how well tools interoperate.
  • Co-dependence: how a change to a shape, a mapping or a module ripples through the rest.

Why now

AI agents enter the workflow

Where does the ontologist's judgment remain irreplaceable, and how can domain experts, users and ontologists stay central rather than become an afterthought to the pipeline?

RDF 1.2 and SHACL 1.2 arrive

New ways to represent, query and validate the very artefacts this lifecycle produces. A map drawn today without them would already be out of date.

The connective tissue is missing

The technologies are mature and the artefacts pile up in every project. What's missing is the link between them, and that account can only be written collectively.

Scope

The lifecycle as a connected whole

The survey is organised around the knowledge-engineering lifecycle, not as a set of isolated topics.

Methodologies
Ontology engineering, shape design, mapping design, and how they integrate.
Core technologies
OWL, SHACL, RML and SPARQL, as combined in practice, not only in specification.
Reusable building blocks
Shape libraries, ODPs, modules, domain ontologies and thesauri: publication, discovery, reuse.
Design-time documentation
Competency questions, requirements, pattern rationales, and their survival over time.
Cartography of validation
Validation reports, reasoner outputs, query results, transformation and migration logs as one trace.
Quality evaluation
Graph-based, ontology-focused and SHACL-assisted metrics, and how they are applied.
Tools and interfaces
Software and UIs supporting any of the above, mapped against the tasks they cover.
Co-dependence
How tasks and technologies interact: no part of the lifecycle can be evaluated in isolation.

Take part

How it works for participants

A small group, deliberately: researchers already active in some part of this space, willing to commit to the annotation work, not just a co-author byline. Designed to fit alongside an existing research load.

Option 1

Join an existing cluster

Shapes, Ontology & methodology, Mapping, Quality or Surveys: pick the one closest to your work.

Option 2

Propose a new cluster

A topic the grid doesn't cover as its own cluster yet, e.g. SPARQL tooling, competency questions, OWL profiles and reasoning.

Option 3

Join the collective writing

A cluster's synthesis, the cross-cutting gaps and frontier section, or general coordination.

Then, in each cluster

  1. Cluster set-up

    Members gather in their cluster; a proposed cluster gets its own sub-library, collected with the survey pipeline.

  2. Grid validation

    Each cluster finalises the annotation fields for its own area; you're best placed to judge your own sub-domain.

  3. First filter

    A quick relevance pass over the cluster's slice of the corpus, a few minutes per paper.

  4. Annotation

    Retained papers get the full grid, with more than one annotator per entry so we can measure agreement.

  5. Collective writing

    Each cluster drafts the synthesis for its area, then a joint editing pass, open to members who joined for the writing phase.

Time commitment

Weekly commitment~1 hour
Trial period1 month (about 4 hours)
Full programme, if the cluster continuesup to 6 months
Scopeset after the trial, based on who stays

The first month is a genuine trial: validating your cluster's grid and annotating a first handful of papers. At the end of it, the cluster decides together how far its corpus extends.

As a rough guide, with around 20 people staying the course we can screen close to 2,000 papers and fully annotate around 240. With fewer people, we scope down rather than stretch the same commitment thinner. Nobody signs up for six months of uncertain scope on day one.

Plan and progress

Five phases, built collectively

  1. Frame the space and gather the communityA crowdsourced first list of methodologies and tools, reflecting the field as the group sees it, not one lab's view.
  2. Define and validate the gridPiloted on a shared set of papers to check it is usable and consistent across annotators.
  3. Collect and annotateA structured, shared, checkable record of what each paper covers, claims and demonstrates.
  4. Assess advancement and gapsWhere the field has advanced, where claims outpace evidence, where the lifecycle is weakly connected.
  5. Identify the frontierResearch lines that follow from the gaps, notably agentic and automated curation and validation.

Systematic methodology

A systematic review, built collectively and extended on demand

The literature is collected, screened and annotated with SciLEx, an open-source Python toolbox for systematic reviews. Every step leaves a trace in the shared Zotero library, so any annotation can be traced back to its paper, questioned and corrected.

  • Explicit queries Keyword sets, sources and year ranges are written down, not typed ad hoc.
  • Documented screening Relevance, quality and citation scores are recorded on each paper.
  • Reproducible pipeline Collection, deduplication, enrichment and annotation are scripted and re-runnable.
  • Reviewed annotations Closed vocabularies, agreement between annotators, a flag on anything still to check.
  1. 1 · CollectQuery scholarly APIs

    Semantic Scholar, OpenAlex, DBLP, arXiv, HAL, IEEE, Springer, Elsevier, Istex, plus existing surveys.

  2. 2 · ScreenMerge and screen

    Deduplicated, screened on title and abstract, scores recorded, DOIs resolved.

  3. 3 · CurateShared library

    Relevant papers filed by theme in the Zotero group.

  4. 4 · EnrichMetadata and citations

    Missing metadata from OpenAlex and Crossref; the corpus grows along citations.

  5. 5 · AnnotateThe annotation grid

    Annotations are written back to Zotero as tags and notes; agreement is measured.

  6. 6 · AnalyseAdvances and gaps

    Annotations are queried across the corpus to compare and locate gaps.

A living survey

New clusters, on demand

When a member proposes a new cluster (SHACL 1.2 and RDF 1.2 artefacts, agentic curation, validation reports, an application domain…), the same pipeline runs again with a focused query: the papers land in a new sub-library and are annotated with the same grid.

The annotation grid

Each paper describes one or more entities, recorded in four tables. Fields use closed vocabularies, and entries link to the ones they extend, implement or reuse, so the grid reads as a connected graph, not a spreadsheet.

Table A

Methodologies

Process paradigm, lifecycle phases, actors, role of LLMs, formalisms, validation, tooling.

Table B

Resources

Libraries of patterns, shapes, modules and ontologies: origin, formalism, link with shapes, access.

Table C

Quality methods

Contribution type, target level, quality dimensions, formalism evaluated, implementing tools.

Table D

Tools

Category, languages, interface, editing style, import and export, licence, access status.

For annotators: how annotations are stored in Zotero

A field value becomes a tag such as KE_LANGUAGES:SHACL, the entity a paper describes becomes KE_ID:T:Protege, and relations become links such as LINK_usesTool:T:Protege. Drafted or uncertain annotations carry WF_ToCheck until a second annotator has reviewed them.
